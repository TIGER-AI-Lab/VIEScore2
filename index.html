TL;DR

Most image evaluators give you a single score but don't show where the image goes wrong. VIEScore2 is one model that scores generated and edited images and marks the defective regions on a 16×16 grid in a single pass. A simple rule-based parser then turns those predictions into a readable explanation.

Because the grid is plain text, it can be trained with SFT and then GRPO using rewards checked directly against ground truth. The result agrees with human ratings better than general-purpose VLMs such as GPT and Gemini under matched inputs, and is competitive with specialized spatial evaluators at localizing defects.

Unified scoring, localization & explanation

One model handles generation and editing with zero, one, or multiple conditioning images. It outputs scores and defect locations without task-specific heads; a parameter-free parser turns them into spatially grounded feedback.

Verifiable spatial post-training

Defects are sparse cells on a text-native grid, so outputs are directly parseable. GRPO with a cell-level Dice reward optimizes localization overlap without a learned reward model.

Heterogeneous supervision

Scores, masks, and heatmaps from five sources are normalized into one format, keeping only the supervision each example actually has.

Method

Masks and heatmaps from ImagenWorld, RichHF-18K, PAL4VST and COCO, together with score-only data from EvalMuse-40K, are converted into one training format: scores on a 0–10 scale and defect cells on an N×N grid, written as text (e.g. r5: 8,9 marks columns 8–9 of row 5). Qwen3-VL-8B is first trained with supervised fine-tuning, then with GRPO using verifiable rewards for cell-level Dice overlap, score accuracy, and output format.

At inference, a parameter-free parser groups the predicted defect cells, describes their locations, and links artifacts to perceptual quality and misalignments to semantic consistency, so explanations stay faithful to the predictions by construction.

VIEScore2 in Action

A held-out RichHF example, end to end: the model's raw output lists scores and defect cells row by row (! marks cells with high annotation coverage), and the parameter-free parser turns it into the explanation on the right.

The same model handles all six ImagenWorld tasks: text-to-image generation (TIG), text-guided editing (TIE), and generation/editing with one (SRIG/SRIE) or multiple (MRIG/MRIE) conditioning images.

Quality Scoring across Tasks

With matched inputs (generated image, prompt, and any conditioning images), VIEScore2 achieves the highest aggregate overall-score SRCC. ImagenWorld covers text-to-image generation (TIG), text-guided editing (TIE), and generation/editing with one (SRIG/SRIE) or multiple conditioning images (MRIG/MRIE). In joint evaluation, VIEScore2 also reaches 0.324 grid IoU and 0.506 F1 with PQ/SC SRCC of 0.558/0.564, ahead of Qwen3-VL-8B and GPT-5.6-terra on every metric.

Analysis

  • GRPO beats more SFT. Across three runs, GRPO improves grid IoU by 0.024–0.029 over the shared SFT checkpoint; an extra SFT epoch gives only 0.001.
  • The Dice reward does the work. Dice alone raises grid IoU from 0.296 to 0.320; the full reward reaches 0.324 IoU and 0.506 F1.
  • Conditioning images help. On the same 250 conditional examples, giving the model the source/reference images raises overall-score SRCC from 0.420 to 0.451 and localization F1 from 0.514 to 0.536, with everything else held fixed.
  • N=16 balances fidelity and length. Trained pixel-IoU is similar at N=12 and N=16 (0.235/0.231) and drops at N=32 (0.192). N=16 preserves annotations better than N=12 (0.615 vs. 0.535) while keeping 95th-percentile targets far shorter than N=32 (471 vs. 1,926 tokens).

Limitations

  • Coarse grid. A 16×16 grid can miss small defects and only roughly follows irregular boundaries.
  • Faithful is not the same as correct. The parser's explanations faithfully reflect the predicted scores and grid, but don't check whether those predictions are right or explain why a defect occurs.
  • False alarms. GRPO can increase false alarms on some clean-image subsets, trading conservative clean-image recognition for stronger localization.
  • One backbone. VIEScore2 is validated only with Qwen3-VL-8B; other backbones and scales remain untested.
